AGENCY: Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), DOT.

ACTION: Notice of proposed rulemaking (NPRM).

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: The FAA proposes to adopt a new airworthiness directive (AD) 
for all CFM International, S.A. (CFM) Model CFM56-7B engines. This 
proposed AD was prompted by an event involving a CFM Model CFM56-7B26/3 
engine where a fan blade separated and resulted in an engine in-flight 
shutdown (IFSD) with debris penetrating the fuselage, which caused a 
loss of pressurization and prompted an emergency descent. This proposed 
AD would require a visual inspection of the fan blades for findings 
considered not serviceable and, depending on the results of the 
inspection, replacement with a serviceable part and additional fan 
blade inspections. This proposed AD would also require sending the 
inspection results to CFM if any finding considered not serviceable is 
found. The FAA is proposing this AD to address the unsafe condition on 
these products.

Background

    The FAA received a report that a Boeing 737-800 Model airplane 
powered by CFM56-7B26/3 engines experienced a fan blade separation 
during flight that resulted in an engine IFSD with debris penetrating 
the fuselage, which caused a loss of pressurization and prompted an 
emergency descent. The manufacturer investigation into the root cause 
of the fan blade failure is ongoing, but preliminary investigation 
indicates deformation to the fan blades induced a localized vibratory 
response in the failed blade. This condition, if not addressed, could 
result in debris penetrating the fuselage and cause a loss of 
pressurization and prompt an emergency descent.
    On April 17, 2018, a Southwest Airlines flight experienced a CFM56-
7B engine failure due to a fractured fan blade that led to the engine 
inlet cowl

[[Page 67533]]

disintegrating and debris penetrating the fuselage. The FAA has 
received no factual information that indicates that the April 17, 2018 
incident on a Southwest Airlines flight is related to the unsafe 
condition addressed by this proposed AD. It is noted here for 
completeness only.
    The FAA is providing a 30-day comment period for this NPRM instead 
of the standard 45-day comment period. This shortened timeframe is 
necessary to align the effective implementation of this safety action 
with the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA), which is the 
Technical Agent for the Member States of the European Union, and in 
consideration of the recent in-service engine event. The FAA has 
determined that a 30-day comment period provides sufficient opportunity 
for public input while ensuring harmonization with EASA.

FAA's Determination

    The FAA is issuing this NPRM after determining that the unsafe 
condition described previously is likely to exist or develop on other 
products of the same type design.

Material Incorporated by Reference Under 1 CFR Part 51

    The FAA reviewed CFM Service Bulletin CFM56-7B S/B 72-1119, dated 
September 8, 2026, which specifies procedures for visual inspection of 
the fan blades for findings considered not serviceable (cracks, tears, 
tip curls, local distortion on leading edge), and additional 
inspections of adjacent (forward looking aft) fan blades. This material 
is reasonably available because the interested parties have access to 
it through their normal course of business or by the means identified 
in the ADDRESSES section.

Proposed AD Requirements in This NPRM

    This proposed AD would require a visual inspection of the fan 
blades for findings considered not serviceable (cracks, tears, tip 
curls, local distortion on leading edge) and, depending on the results 
of the inspection, replacement with a serviceable part and additional 
fan blade inspections. This proposed AD would also require sending the 
inspection results to CFM if any unserviceable finding is found.

Interim Action

    This AD is considered to be interim action. The inspection reports 
that are required by this AD will enable the manufacturer to obtain 
better insight into the nature, cause, and extent of the fan blade 
failure, and eventually allow the manufacturer to develop final action 
to address the unsafe condition. Once final action has been identified, 
the FAA might consider further rulemaking.

Authority for This Rulemaking

    Title 49 of the United States Code specifies the FAA's authority to 
issue rules on aviation safety. Subtitle I, section 106, describes the 
authority of the FAA Administrator. Subtitle VII: Aviation Programs, 
describes in more detail the scope of the Agency's authority.
    The FAA is issuing this rulemaking under the authority described in 
Subtitle VII, Part A, Subpart III, Section 44701: General requirements. 
Under that section, Congress charges the FAA with promoting safe flight 
of civil aircraft in air commerce by prescribing regulations for 
practices, methods, and procedures the Administrator finds necessary 
for safety in air commerce. This regulation is within the scope of that 
authority because it addresses an unsafe condition that is likely to 
exist or develop on products identified in this rulemaking action.

(d) Subject

    Joint Aircraft System Component (JASC) Code 7230, Turbine Engine 
Compressor Section.

(e) Unsafe Condition

    This AD was prompted by an event involving a CFM Model CFM56-
7B26/3 engine, which experienced a fan blade separation that 
resulted in an in-flight engine shutdown and debris penetrating the 
fuselage, causing a loss of pressurization and prompting an 
emergency descent. The FAA is issuing this AD to prevent failure of 
the fan blade. The unsafe condition, if not addressed, could result 
in debris penetrating the fuselage and cause a loss of 
pressurization and prompt an emergency descent.

(f) Compliance

    Comply with this AD within the compliance times specified, 
unless already done.

(g) Definition

    For the purpose of this AD, a ``serviceable fan blade'' is a 
new, zero-hour fan blade or a fan blade that has passed the 
inspections required by paragraph (h)(1) of this AD.

(h) Required Actions

    (1) Perform a visual inspection of the fan blades in accordance 
with the Accomplishment Instructions, paragraph 3.C., of CFM Service 
Bulletin (SB) CFM56-7B S/B 72-1119, dated September 8, 2026 (CFM56-
7B S/B 72-1119), as follows:
    (i) For engines installed on aircraft as of the effective date 
of this AD, within 400 flight cycles or within 90 days after the 
effective date of this AD, whichever occurs first.
    (ii) For engines not installed on aircraft as of the effective 
date of this AD, before further flight.
    (2) If any findings considered not serviceable are detected, 
before further flight, do the following:
    (i) Remove the fan blade from service and replace with a 
serviceable fan blade.
    (ii) Perform an inspection of the adjacent clockwise (forward 
looking aft) fan blade for findings considered not serviceable in 
accordance with the Accomplishment Instructions, paragraph 3.D.(2), 
of CFM56-7B S/B 72-1119.
    (iii) If any unserviceable finding is found during the 
inspection required by paragraph (h)(2)(ii) of this AD, before 
further flight, remove the fan blade from service and replace with a 
serviceable fan blade.

(i) Credit for Previous Actions

    This paragraph provides credit for actions required by paragraph 
(h) of this AD, if those actions were performed prior to the 
effective date of this AD using the material identified in, and 
meeting the applicable conditions specified in, paragraphs (i)(1) 
through (3) of this AD.
    (1) Credit is allowed for the actions required by paragraph (h) 
of this AD if an engine shop manual piece-part inspection was 
performed on each fan blade in the set within 100 flight cycles 
prior to the effective date of this AD; or
    (2) Credit is allowed if the actions required by paragraph (h) 
of this AD were performed within 100 flight cycles prior to the 
effective date of this AD using CFM SB CFM56-7B S/B 72-1033, 
Revision 3, dated November 6, 2018; CFM56-7B S/B 72-1033, Revision 
4, dated September 12, 2019; CFM56-7B S/B 72-1033, Revision 5, dated 
July 28, 2026; or CFM56-7B S/B 72-1033, Revision 6, dated August 14, 
2026; or
    (3) Credit is allowed if the actions required by paragraph (h) 
of this AD were performed using CFM SB CFM56-7B S/B 72-1118, dated 
June 1, 2026.

(j) Reporting Requirement

    Report the results of the inspections required by paragraph (h) 
of this AD to CFM within 30 days after the inspection or within 30 
days after the effective date of this AD, whichever occurs later, in 
accordance with the Accomplishment Instructions, paragraph 3.D.(3), 
of CFM56-7B S/B 72-1119.
